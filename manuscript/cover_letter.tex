\noindent To the Editors, \emph{Briefings in Bioinformatics}

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{Re: ``Morphometric validity is not a scalar: segmentation
accuracy does not predict which biological conclusions survive
measurement''}

\vspace{0.4em}
Dear Editors,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Problem Solving Protocol.

Its claim is that the segmentation metrics the field uses to choose a
method do not certify the biological measurements derived from that method,
and we quantify by how much. On LIVECell, foreground
intersection-over-union rises from 0.832 to 0.905 as fields fill while
matched-instance detection F1 on that same predicted foreground collapses
from 0.416 to 0.017: the pixel score gives no warning of the instance-level
failure beneath it. The consequence is not academic. Cellpose detects
significantly better than the lightweight extension we introduce (F1 0.815
against 0.709, non-overlapping intervals) yet returns no detectable
morphological trajectory for BT-474, one of the best-evidenced lineages in
the dataset, where the weaker detector recovers one. Which lineages a
pipeline gets wrong depends on the segmenter, and detection accuracy does
not predict which.

The design is built to make that comparison interpretable rather than
suggestive. Four mask sources spanning a sevenfold range in detection F1
are passed through one identical geometry code path, so any difference in
the biology recovered is attributable to the masks. Operating points are
selected on validation and frozen before the test split is touched, with
the resulting degradation reported rather than hidden. Every result carries
bootstrap confidence intervals and paired tests, and is checked against
four independent artifact controls. An ablation isolates the mechanism to a
single 33-parameter head, and the training runs reproduce to bit-identical
weight hashes. All code, per-cell measurements and trained weights are
released.

The question is live in this journal. \emph{Briefings in Bioinformatics}
has recently published two systematic evaluations of cell segmentation
(\texttt{bbae407}; \texttt{bbag066}), both of which rank methods by
segmentation quality and motivate that ranking by the biological analyses
the masks will feed. Neither tests whether the ranking predicts the
validity of those analyses. That is the gap this paper closes, and it
requires a design neither of them needed. The journal has also published
the argument that benchmark competitiveness and generalization are not
equivalent properties (\texttt{bbag193}); our contribution is the
measurement of that separation for bioimage-derived morphometry, against
expert ground truth, across a continuous crowding gradient.
